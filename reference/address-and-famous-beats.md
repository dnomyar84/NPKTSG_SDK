## Address culture (British / Hogwarts)

British formality tracks closeness and audience. A first name is a private door. A title is the public one. Hogwarts is a boarding school, so the door stays shut in class, in the Great Hall, and in any corridor where other people can hear.

### Students speaking to a teacher

In class, in the corridor, at a feast, or anywhere another person can hear, a student says **Professor** plus the surname, or **Professor** alone when it is already obvious who is being spoken to. That is the whole form.

Never a teacher's first name. Never the surname alone to their face. Harry does not say "Snape" to Snape. He does not say "Albus" to Dumbledore. Ron does not say "McGonagall" at her. Hermione does not drop the title to earn a laugh.

Not every adult is a professor. Madam Hooch, Madam Pomfrey, and Madam Pince are **Madam**. Filch is **Mr Filch**. The Professor rule is for the teaching staff.

### Students speaking about a teacher

In private gossip, complaint, or fear, the surname alone is natural. "Snape." "Dumbledore." "McGonagall." That is how British children talk once the teacher has left the room. It is not the same act as saying it to the teacher's face.

Hermione often keeps **Professor** even in private. She is formal when she is worried, not only when she is showing off. Ron drops the title more readily. Harry uses the bare surname for Snape when he is angry or afraid in private. He still says **Professor Dumbledore**, even in private and at the bedside, out of respect. Do not flatten those three habits into one.

### First names

First names are for people who are close, or for a conversation that is genuinely private and personal.

Close: Harry, Ron, and Hermione with one another. Hagrid with the children. Family. Molly can use Harry's first name because she has taken him in. The Dursleys use first names because they are family, however badly.

Not close, so not a first name: a classmate who is only a rival, a teacher in public, a stranger in Diagon Alley, the whole school at a feast. Draco saying "Potter" is an insult between boys, not the teacher rule. Friends use first names. Hostility uses surnames.

Using someone's first name in front of a crowd, without that closeness, reads as rude or as a claim of intimacy the speaker has not earned.

### Teachers speaking to students

In public, and in front of the school, teachers use the surname forms. **Mr Potter**, **Potter**, **Miss Granger**, **Mr Weasley**, **Mr Longbottom**.

Snape in public is cutting. **Potter** or **Mr Potter**. Not a warm Harry.

Dumbledore at a feast or in the Great Hall uses the public name. **Harry Potter**, or the formal **Mr Potter** / **Mr Harry Potter** when he is awarding points or presenting the boy to the room. He does not chat to him as Harry from the high table.

McGonagall in public is stern and exact. **Potter** when she is icy. **Miss Granger**, **Mr Weasley**, **Mr Longbottom** when she is teaching. She is not Snape, and she is not familiar.

### The hospital wing

The bedside is the exception. Dumbledore speaks to him as **Harry** even if Hermione and Ron are in the room. It is a quiet personal visit, not a speech to the school. The friends' presence does not turn the chair beside the bed into the Great Hall.

Harry still answers **Professor** or **Professor Dumbledore**.

The current panels stage this as two visits: Dumbledore alone at the bed, then the friends after he has gone. The first-name greeting is already there for the private visit. The user's shape is the stricter one to keep in mind later: the first name still holds if the friends are around. Do not "correct" it back to Mr Potter because Ron and Hermione can hear.

### Staff with each other

In front of other people, McGonagall says **Professor Dumbledore**. The storyboard already has her use that form on the doorstep. Keep it whenever a student, a colleague, or a crowd can hear.

In private, McGonagall may say **Albus**. That is the same rule as anyone else: the first name is for a closed conversation between people who have known each other for years. Do not put it in a corridor full of children.

Hagrid says **Professor Dumbledore**. He uses first names for the children. He does not get familiar with the headmaster in front of them.

Snape and the other teachers keep titles in front of students. Quirrell's timid mask is **Professor Quirrell** to the children. When the mask drops, he uses **Potter**, the way a hostile adult drops warmth, not the way a friend drops a title.

### What later rewrites must not do

Do not invent lines to demonstrate these rules. Change only the form of address when a line is already being rewritten. If a panel has no speech, leave it without speech.
