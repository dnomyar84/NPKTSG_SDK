# Character locks

Paste the paragraph. Species, size, colors, and marks stay put. Clothes are in [wardrobes.md](wardrobes.md). Objects are in [props.md](props.md).

Do not add thestrals. They are not in this film. Do not add a house-elf. Dobby is not in this story.

## Harry

A small eleven-year-old boy, thin, messy jet-black hair that sticks up and will not lie flat, green eyes, round wire glasses taped at the bridge before he can replace them. The lightning scar is on his anatomical right brow, viewer’s left when he faces the camera, a jagged cut from the brow up into the hair. It never switches sides. He is much smaller than Hagrid and smaller than the older Quidditch players. Baby Harry is the same black tuft and the same scar, fresh, wrapped in a soft green blanket.

## Ron

Tall and thin, bright red hair, freckles across the nose, a long nose, ears that go red. Secondhand everything. He is a child, not a teenager yet. Same face from the barrier to the train home.

## Hermione

Bushy brown hair that gets bigger when it is wet or singed, large front teeth, a precise face, brown eyes. She is about Harry’s height. Same girl when she is furious, when she is crying, and when she is reading.

## Draco, Crabbe, Goyle

Draco: pale, pointed chin, slicked platinum-blond hair, gray eyes, a small superior mouth. Crabbe and Goyle: two different bulky boys, dark hair, heavy faces, almost no expression. Do not merge them into one bodyguard, and do not redraw them as twins. The twins are Fred and George.

## Neville

Round face, sandy hair, ears that go red, a bit shorter and softer than Harry. Same boy when he is terrified and when he is lifted at the feast.

## Hagrid

A half-giant, more than twice a first-year’s height. His head nearly touches a cottage door and the hut’s bulb. Huge shoulders, wild black hair, a tangled black beard, beetle-black eyes, a kind wide face, enormous hands. He can cry. He is not a monster and not a normal tall man.

## Dumbledore

Very tall and thin, older than the other teachers, long silver hair and a long silver beard, half-moon spectacles, a crooked nose, kind eyes. The tallest person in a room except when Hagrid is there. Hagrid is broader. Dumbledore is the thin one.

## McGonagall

A severe woman, sharp face, dark hair scraped into a tight bun, square spectacles, thin mouth. She is tall for a teacher and still shorter than Dumbledore.

### Tabby Animagus

The same cat every time she is a cat. A thin brown tabby, dark stripes, yellow-amber eyes, no collar. Dark markings around the eyes read as spectacle frames, the same shape as her square glasses. On the wall she is small against the street. On a desk she is an ordinary cat, not a panther. When she changes, the eyes stay, the spectacle marks become the glasses, and the robes settle. No wand line. Not Mrs Norris. Not a black cat. Not a different tabby.

## Snape

Sallow skin, a large hooked nose, greasy shoulder-length black hair, black eyes, thin mouth. He rarely blinks when he looks at Harry. Adult height, black robes. The same face when he is the suspect and when he was actually protecting Harry.

## Quirrell, turban on

Pale, nervous, dark hair only at the edge of a large purple turban, wide worried eyes, a stammer in the shoulders and hands. Hunched. Purple robes. This is the man in class, at feasts, and in the corridor. The turban stays on until the last chamber.

## Quirrell, turban off, and the face

Same man, same purple robes, but the shoulders are straight and the stammer is gone. The turban is unwound in his hands or gone. On the back of his skull, where the cloth was, a second face: flat, pale, no hair, slit nostrils, red eyes, a mouth. It is part of the head, not a mask he holds and not a second body. Harry sees it when Quirrell turns. The front face and the back face are both Quirrell’s head. Do not give Voldemort his own legs.

## Hooded figure in the forest

A tall hunched shape in a tattered dark cloak, hood up, no readable face, only a blackness and a glint. Pale hands. Silver unicorn blood at the mouth. This is Voldemort not yet restored. Do not draw the skull-face clearly under the hood. The readable face comes later, on Quirrell. Do not draw a thestral, a snake body, or the adult Voldemort of a later film.

In the memory of the night James and Lily died, Voldemort is not shown. Only green light.

## James and Lily

In the Mirror, and only as that memory: James has messy black hair and round glasses, an adult Harry without the scar. Lily has long dark-red hair and kind eyes, a simple dress or robe. The same two faces every time Harry looks. If the panel asks for more family, they stay soft behind those two. Do not invent a new mother.

## Dudley, Vernon, Petunia

Dudley: blond, round, about Harry’s age, much heavier, small eyes. Vernon: big, almost no neck, black mustache, purple when shouting. Petunia: thin, long neck, horse-like face, pale, blonde hair pinned tight. They are a Muggle family. They do not gain wizard clothes.

## Weasley family

Molly: plump, red hair, warm face. Fred and George: identical red-haired teenagers, same face twice. Percy: older, tidy, a prefect badge. Ginny: a small red-haired child, not yet at school. Ron is the young one with the long nose and the rat.

## Nearly Headless Nick

A ghost, pearly bluish-white, transparent, an Elizabethan ruff and doublet. The neck is almost severed, so the head tips and wobbles but does not fall off. He has a full figure down to feet that fade. He is not a floating head and not a solid man. He can pass through a table. Same ruff, same wound, every time. Sir Nicholas, polite, pleased to be noticed.

## Other ghosts

Any ghost in this book is pearly, bluish-white, and transparent, with fading feet, the same treatment as Nick. The Bloody Baron and the Grey Lady are not in this storyboard. Do not add them to fill a house table. Houses are shown with tables, ties, banners, and hourglasses.

A painted wizard in a frame is a portrait, not a ghost. He has painted edges and a frame. He is not pearly.

## The Fat Lady

One woman in one painting. Plump, pink silk dress, period curls, a gold frame, the portrait hole into Gryffindor. She can lean out of the frame and swing aside. She does not change dress, hair, frame, or body. Same painting in the midnight corridor, after Hermione runs, and when she is dozing.

## Peeves

He was cut from the finished film. This storyboard still uses him, once, in the corridor before Transfiguration. Lock that design and reuse it if he returns: a small poltergeist, not a ghost, solid enough to hold a wastebasket. Orange and purple jester motley, a belled hat, tongue out, cruel grin. He hangs from a chandelier. He is not pearly and not a student.

## Nicolas Flamel

He does not walk into any scene. He is a small engraved portrait of an old man beside his name in an alchemy book: lined face, long beard, a simple old robe or cap, woodcut lines. The same engraving if the page is shown again. Chocolate Frog cards in this storyboard are Dumbledore’s, not Flamel’s. Do not put Flamel’s face on the card, and do not bring him to the hospital wing.

## Hedwig

A snowy owl, female, large, white with faint dark barring on the wings, gold eyes, a black beak. The same owl in the shop, on the train, in the dormitory, and in the cage at King’s Cross. Not a barn owl and not a tawny. The owl that drops the first letter in the Dursley kitchen is a different, ordinary tawny, and it leaves. Hedwig stays.

## Scabbers

Ron’s old rat. Fat, gray, sleepy, a bald patch on the back, a long pink scabby tail, yellowed look, often asleep on a wrapper or in a pocket. He fills Ron’s two hands. He is not a cute mouse, not a sleek young rat, and not white. Same rat all year. On the train home he can look a little healthier and still be this rat.

## Trevor

Neville’s toad. A common toad, brown-green, lumpy, about the size of a fist, no costume. Same toad. He is not a frog from a Chocolate Frog box.

## Fang

Hagrid’s boarhound. Huge, black, drooling, a coward, bigger than a first-year, much smaller than Fluffy. One head. He hides under tables. He is not the three-headed dog.

## Mrs Norris

Filch’s cat. Thin, pale sandy-gray, a squashed face, lamp-like eyes, a collar, usually at his boots. She is not the tabby and she has no spectacle marks. Uncertain of the exact film eye color. Do not give her a new breed per scene.

## Fluffy

One dog, three heads, the size of the third-floor room. Shaggy brown, the same brown every time, dark leather collars, drool, amber eyes, huge teeth, paws the size of doors, black claws. Left head, middle head, right head, one body. Not three dogs standing together. Asleep, a head gets heavy lids and a small Zzz. Awake heads do not. Music notes from the flute are in [flute-and-fluffy.md](flute-and-fluffy.md).

## Mountain troll

One design for the whole book. Huge, gray with a greenish shade in shadow, a small head, tiny dull eyes, a huge belly, long arms, bare feet, a filthy hide loincloth, a wooden club like a young tree. He fills a doorway and is taller than the bathroom stalls. The smell is wavy lines and the children’s faces, not a different body.

The troll in the underground room is this same creature, already knocked out: on his back, club loose, tongue out, snoring. Do not invent a second species, a smaller troll, or a troll in armor.

## Norbert

A baby Norwegian Ridgeback. Black-bronze scales, spines along the back, a gold eye, a thread of smoke from the nostrils, claws too sharp, wings small and stuck down at hatching. The head is about the size of a cat when he breaks the shell. Within days he is the size of a wolfhound and the hut is too small. He leaves in a crate, one gold eye at a knothole, a spiny tail that will not fit under the cloak. Same dragon at every size. Not a green Western dragon and not a full-grown ridgeback.

## Unicorn

A slender white horse with one spiraled horn, a pale mane, and a faint silver-white shine. The dead unicorn in the clearing is that same animal: white, slender, the horn still there but dim, silver blood pooling and luminous. Not gory. Not a deer. Not a thestral. There is no living herd to redesign. If another unicorn is shown, she matches this one.

## Firenze

A palomino centaur. Human torso, no shirt, long blond hair, a calm stern young face, a bow on his back. The horse half is cream-gold, one body, four horse legs. Harry is small on that back. Same centaur at the charge and at the stream. Not a shirt, not a different coat color, not a second centaur with a new face unless the storyboard names one. This storyboard uses Firenze.

## Portraits that are not people in the room

The Fat Lady, Nicolas’s engraving, and the angry little wizard who leans out of a frame on a landing are paintings. They keep their frames. They are not ghosts and not actors who have stepped fully into the corridor, except for the lean the storyboard already allows.
